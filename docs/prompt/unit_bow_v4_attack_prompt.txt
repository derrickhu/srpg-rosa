CRITICAL BACKGROUND REQUIREMENT, THIS OVERRIDES EVERYTHING ELSE: the background must be 100% solid
flat MAGENTA, hex #FF00FF. NOT black, NOT white, NOT grey, NOT a gradient. Every pixel that is not
the character must be exactly magenta #FF00FF, including between the cells. It is a chroma key.

The attached reference image contains ONE SINGLE character frame. It defines only the CHARACTER
IDENTITY and the ART STYLE, NOT the layout. Do NOT copy the reference's framing and do NOT produce
a walk cycle — produce the 2x2 attack sheet described below.

Match the reference exactly on: the same cheerful chibi forest archer, same leaf-green hood and
tunic, same cream off-white cape, same warm reddish-brown hair, same big honey-wood bow with a pale
bowstring, same thick near-black outlines, same flat fills with light cel shading, same
giant-head-tiny-body proportions.

A 2x2 sprite sheet: 4 frames of a BOW SHOT. <朝向段落>. The draw and release is the whole point —
the bowstring and his arms must be the things that move, and the change between frames must be
obvious in silhouette.

  朝左：the character seen in LEFT SIDE PROFILE, facing LEFT the whole time and shooting toward the
        left. He stays in LEFT profile in all 4 frames — never turn him toward the camera, never
        flip him to face right.
  朝下：the character FACING DOWN toward the camera the whole time, shooting toward the viewer. His
        face stays visible in every frame. He holds the bow UPRIGHT in front of his chest, seen
        flat-on from the front so its full D shape is visible, and draws the string toward himself.
  朝上：the character seen FROM BEHIND, facing UP and AWAY from the camera the whole time, shooting
        away from the viewer. His face is never visible. He holds the bow upright, seen flat-on
        from behind so its full D shape reads.

FRAME ORDER, left-to-right then top-to-bottom:
  Frame 1 (top-left):     RAISE. He lifts the bow up in front of him, arm extended, string still
                          relaxed and straight, one arrow nocked.
  Frame 2 (top-right):    DRAW. He hauls the string far BACK past his cheek — the string bends into
                          a deep sharp V and the bow limbs visibly flex. His rear elbow juts back
                          behind his body. This is the most recognisable archery pose; make the V
                          deep and unmistakable.
  Frame 3 (bottom-left):  RELEASE. The string has snapped back to straight, his drawing hand flies
                          open beside his cheek, his body recoils slightly, and a single arrow is
                          leaving the bow, its tip clear of his front hand.
  Frame 4 (bottom-right): RECOVERY. He lowers the bow back to a neutral carry at his side, string
                          relaxed, body straightening.

ABSOLUTE RULES:
1. Background is 100% solid flat magenta #FF00FF everywhere including between cells. No gradient,
   no vignette, no shadow.
2. EXACTLY 4 equal-size cells in a 2x2 grid, every cell the same width and height.
3. NO borders, NO dividing lines, NO gutters, NO frames between cells.
4. IDENTICAL BODY SCALE and IDENTICAL camera distance in all 4 cells. Measure him from the top of
   his hood to the soles of his boots, ignoring the bow: that height must be the same in all 4
   cells. Do not zoom in on the action.
5. HIS BODY (hood-top to boots, bow excluded) FILLS ABOUT 55% OF THE CELL HEIGHT. Draw him
   generously big in the cell — a tiny figure lost in a huge empty cell is WRONG.
6. Nothing crosses a cell edge. The bow tips and the flying arrow must stay clear of the edges.
7. Feet rest on the same horizontal baseline in every cell.
8. Draw the CHARACTER AND HIS ARROW ONLY. NO motion-blur streaks, NO speed lines, NO energy trail,
   NO glow, NO impact flash, NO dust.
9. NO text, no labels, no numbers, no UI pointers, no watermark.
